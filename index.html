About Me

I study the impact that artificial intelligence has on organizations and the people who work in them.

Hello! I am a PhD Candidate in Decisions, Operations and Technology Management at UCLA Anderson, advised by Charles Corbett and Auyon Siddiq. I will finish my PhD in June 2027, and I am on the academic job market this year.

Before my PhD I spent eight years in industry: four and a half at Oliver Wyman in Madrid and New York, with an MBA at MIT Sloan in between that the firm sponsored, and four at ACHS, a Chilean non-profit that insures half of the country's workforce against workplace accidents. There I built the advanced analytics team from five people to more than fifty. Seeing the potential of those tools, and understanding that someone would have to define frameworks to use them ethically, I decided to take a risk and move with my family to do a PhD.

My research is in empirical operations management. I look for settings where AI changes the nature of work, demands new organizational forms, and affects people's health, safety and habits. Much of my work is on crowdwork platforms, where people produce the data that AI systems learn from. In my job market paper, with Charles Corbett and Andrew Gordon, we use approximately 64 million task submissions from Prolific to show that the frictions new workers face in their first fourteen days hurt the platform primarily through attrition: workers simply leave, often without complaining. This summer I was a student researcher in the Organizational AI Lab at Google DeepMind, studying how agentic AI is changing the way teams work, and I am continuing there part-time until the end of the year. More about my research.

Beyond the research and the data, my life is centered on my family. I am incredibly fortunate to walk this path with my wife, Andrea, and our four children: Carlota, Martín, Joaquín, and Juan Pablo. They are my greatest joy, my daily chaos, and my most important responsibility.
