# Research

<p class="lead scroll-reveal">I look for settings where AI changes the nature of work, demands new organizational forms, and affects people's health, safety and habits.</p>

<p class="scroll-reveal">When I started my PhD in September 2021, ChatGPT had not yet launched, but I was already interested in the impact that algorithms, big data and the ever-expanding computational power of specialized models would have on organizations and on the people in them. I came to that interest from eight years in industry, first in consulting and then at ACHS, where I built the advanced analytics team from five people to more than fifty, analyzing the personal data of millions of workers. I saw how much insight that data could give an organization, and I grew convinced that the ever more sophisticated tools available to firms were going to change the world.</p>

</section>

<section id="to-date" class="section-spacing" markdown="1">

## Where I study it

<p class="scroll-reveal">I started studying <strong>crowdwork platforms</strong> almost by accident, because they were increasingly used to build and train AI models. They are a central component of the supply chain that powers AI systems and makes large-scale academic and commercial research possible. In my first paper, published in <em>Business Horizons</em>, we studied how fairly workers on these platforms feel they are treated, and argued that the debate about ethical AI should include the crowdworkers that AI depends on.</p>

<p class="scroll-reveal">Prolific stood out in that paper as significantly fairer than any other platform we observed, so I wrote to its CEO, who introduced me to the company's research team. My job market paper, <strong>First Impressions Matter: Task Frictions and Retention on a Crowdwork Platform</strong>, is the first project that came out of that relationship. We study two frictions in new workers' first fourteen days: task returns and duration underestimation. Both hurt the platform primarily through attrition rather than through demotivation among the workers who remain. Workers may just leave, often without complaining, opening a support ticket or providing any feedback that the platform can detect.</p>

<p class="scroll-reveal">The longer I have worked on these platforms, the more convinced I have become that they will matter more, not less. Some people believe that synthetic data will make human respondents unnecessary, but I think the opposite is more likely. As AI becomes better at imitating humans, genuine human input will become more valuable, and the platforms that attract and retain honest, reliable workers will lead the market.</p>

<p class="scroll-reveal">My oldest project started at ACHS, before my PhD, from a system my team built to give physicians data-driven guidance on coverage decisions. We first ran it as a pilot in 25 of the insurer's 89 clinics instead of implementing it across all of them. The evidence is consistent with pressure rather than information: physicians granted coverage less often when they saw an alert, but they also avoided the alerts by recoding diagnoses, an unexpected and unintended consequence. As a manager, I insisted on measuring interventions before scaling them, and I still do as a researcher.</p>

<p class="scroll-reveal">This summer I was a student researcher in the Organizational AI Lab at Google DeepMind, working on how agentic AI is changing the way teams work and how their members collaborate. I am continuing there part-time until the end of the year.</p>

</section>

<section id="agenda" class="section-spacing" markdown="1">

## What comes next

<p class="scroll-reveal">Five years later, the question that pushed me into the PhD is more relevant than ever. My research agenda has three streams.</p>

<p class="scroll-reveal"><strong>AI inside organizations.</strong> Organizations are implementing AI and agentic systems at an extremely fast pace, and many have no time to measure how it changes what people do, how they work with each other and how they perform. Instead, they focus on usage metrics that say nothing about any of it. I want to study this by observing behavior at scale rather than surveying perceptions, and to answer questions not only about the technology but also about how organizations should be designed now that AI is part of how work gets done.</p>

<p class="scroll-reveal"><strong>Crowdwork and the quality of human data.</strong> My relationship with Prolific lets me do things that would not be possible on my own. The most advanced of several projects measures the impact of AI on online behavioral research, where some participants now use LLMs to answer studies. In the past, bad quality data was easy to identify. Now a participant can submit long, well-articulated answers that were not written by a human, which is much harder to detect, and the problem affects the data that a large share of the social sciences depends on.</p>

<p class="scroll-reveal"><strong>AI-generated content in the markets it enters.</strong> This stream brings in my personal interests in music and art. Does AI-generated content replace content made by people? Do people value it differently once they know where it comes from? Should platforms disclose it? I want to start with the music industry.</p>

</section>

<section id="approach" class="section-spacing" markdown="1">

## How I work

<p class="scroll-reveal">The research I enjoy most is empirical work on real data from firms and platforms, using the best methods available to answer each question. I believe that AI makes this kind of work more valuable, not less. It is lowering the cost of the technical side of research, so we will spend more of our time on what AI does not do: identifying interesting research questions, finding the data that can help us answer them, understanding the context well enough to interpret what the numbers mean, and building the relationships that make all of this possible. My work with Prolific began with a direct message to its CEO, and the ACHS paper exists because I was leading the team that designed and launched the pilot. Framing a problem well and getting honest data on it is the part that is harder to automate and delegate, and it is the part I intend to be good at.</p>
